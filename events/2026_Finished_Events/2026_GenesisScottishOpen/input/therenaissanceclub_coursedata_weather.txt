The Renaissance Club is technically a "modern links" — Doak built it on a former pine forest, so it lacks the centuries-old natural terrain movement of St. Andrews or Muirfield. What it does have: firm fescue everywhere, pervasive wind exposure, pot bunkers, gorse, and a coastal back nine. By comparison to its East Lothian neighbors (North Berwick West Links, Muirfield, Gullane), it is the least "pure" in links architecture — but under tournament conditions it plays every bit the part.rickrungood
The 2026 Routing Change — KEY UPDATE
This is the single biggest news for 2026 handicapping.instagram
The entire Championship routing has been rerouted for 2026, creating a more dramatic closing stretch for players and spectators. The centerpiece change:
The iconic par-3 "Thistle" hole (previously the 6th in Championship routing) has been moved to play as the NEW 15th hole.irishgolfer
This hole — an ultra-short, wind-lashed par 3 of approximately 147–148 yards — was the "Stadium Hole" in previous years, featuring massive grandstand capacity and seating. In 2025 it was rebranded "The Thistle." In 2026, it now anchors the closing stretch, creating tremendous pressure-cooker theater late in the round. The Thistle Club premium hospitality structure overlooks its green.europeantour
Structural implication: Players who previously faced The Thistle on hole 6 (early in the round, low pressure) now face it on hole 15 — when the tournament is on the line. Shots fired at a 147-yard par 3 over the closing stretch with a Sunday lead carry infinitely more psychological weight. This is a massive edge for players who handle short-game pressure well.bunkered
Additionally, The Renaissance Club has confirmed it will remain the host through 2030.instagram

SECTION A: WHAT TYPE OF GOLF WINS HERE
The Skill-Set Blueprint
The DataGolf radar plot tells the story instantly: The Renaissance Club rewards Approach Play and Putting far above all other skills — both spike well above the PGA Tour average. Driving Distance sits above average as well. Driving Accuracy is the clear bottom-dweller — materially below Tour average. Around the Green sits near neutral.datagolf
The winning formula, distilled:
Long, accurate-enough ball striker who is an elite iron player from 175+ yards, who putts well on slow, undulating fescue surfaces, and who can manage their game intelligently around the wind.
The Five Traits That Repeatedly Show Up in Winners & Elite Finishers
1. ELITE APPROACH PLAY — Specifically from Long Range (175–200+ yards)
This is the single most important skill category at The Renaissance Club. In 2025, 33% of approach shots came from 175–200 yards and 16% from 200+ yards — well above Tour averages for both bands. Every single winner (Gotterup, MacIntyre, McIlroy, Schauffele) was an elite iron ball striker. In 2025, SG:Approach correlation with total was 0.532 — the highest of any category.
Winners who exemplify this: McIlroy (+14.2 SG, 2023 winner), Gotterup (+15.4 SG total, 2025), MacIntyre (course record 262)
The greens are large and tiered — being on the wrong tier from 180 yards is worse than missing a short par 4. Players must flight long irons with purpose.
2. DRIVING DISTANCE (Not Accuracy)
Distance is a clear weapon at Renaissance. Every par 5 is reachable, the drivable par 4 (No. 5) rewards length, and long hitters can carry fairway bunkers placed at 280–310 yards that shorter hitters cannot. In 2025, driving distance correlated 0.273 with SG:Total while driving accuracy correlated only 0.200 — but driving accuracy ranked dead last (37th/38 courses) in missed-fairway penalty in 2022 windy conditions.
The penalty for missing a fairway in 2 inches of fescue rough is near zero. It is only the gorse and extreme misses that cost shots.
Winners who exemplify this: McIlroy (one of Tour's longest), Schauffele (above-average distance), Gotterup (above-average), MacIntyre (above-average for his size)
2025 field avg: 301.0 yards (+8.1 vs Tour avg)
3. PUTTING ON SLOW, UNDULATING FESCUE GREENS
In 2022 (windy conditions), putting accounted for 39.3% of SG — well above Tour average of 35.5%. In 2025, SG:Putting correlation with Total was 0.631 — the highest correlation on the entire board. The greens run at ~10 ft on the Stimpmeter, which is well below Tour standard (~12–13 ft). Players trained on slick Bermuda or bent-grass surfaces who expect the ball to "die" at the hole will chronically over-hit putts. The best putters here are those who:
Putt well from close range under pressure (wind makes 3-footers feel like 6-footers)
Excel at lag putting (huge greens = lots of 40–60 foot putts)
Have experience on UK/Scottish links greens or slow surfaces generally
Winners who exemplify this: All four winners gained significantly with the putter in their winning weeks. Every top-5 finisher in 2022 (the windiest edition) was above average with the flat stick.
4. COMPLETE GAME / BALANCED SKILLSET UNDER PRESSURE
Unlike Augusta (a putting contest) or Harbour Town (accuracy paradise), Renaissance doesn't let you survive on one trick. In 2022, all top-5 players gained strokes in all four major categories — OTT, Approach, ATG, Putting. This is a course that rewards elite overall players — which is why every winner since 2022 has been (at the time) a top-100 world player, and two winners (McIlroy and Schauffele) were top-10 in the world. DataGolf's most DG Points all-time at this event: McIlroy (26.9), MacIntyre (24.7), Schauffele (20.3).
5. LINKS ADAPTABILITY / WIND MANAGEMENT
This is the wild card. Under calm conditions (2019: -22; 2021: -18), Renaissance is a birdie fest — total skill dominates. Under wind (2022: -7; 2023: -15), it becomes a patience-and-management test. Players who have experience on links-style courses — particularly those who know how to flight the ball lower, take more club, keep the ball out of trouble, and commit to bump-and-run short game — gain a systematic edge when the wind arrives. However, 2026 is forecasting relatively calm conditions (see Section B), which shifts weight back toward pure scoring ability rather than wind management.

ANTI-PATTERNS: What Does NOT Work Here
These are the clearest loser profiles at The Renaissance Club:
Anti-PatternExplanation
Driving accuracy specialists without length
Accuracy is penalized ~zero. Short hitters who rely on fairways-hit can't reach par 5s, can't carry bunkers, face longer irons into greens. The 2022 course rated 37th/38 in missed-fairway penalty
Slow-green putting strugglers
Players who putt well on fast Bermuda/bent-grass Tours but fall apart on slow fescue (~10 ft Stimp) consistently disappoint here
Coastal/Bermuda approach specialists
Players who flight the ball high and rely on stopping power struggle on firm fescue greens where balls run through the back
High-ball, spin-dependent iron players
The links-style surface rewards low, penetrating iron play. High soft approaches into firm greens overshoot; proper trajectory management is needed
Poor bunker players
Pot bunkers at Renaissance are among the most punishing on Tour (5th hardest bunker category). Players who can't execute from deep sand (dark, gravelly Scottish sand vs. white American sand) leak shots consistently
Mental fragility under pressure
The closing stretch in 2026 ends with "The Thistle" par 3 at position 15, followed by 3 more hard holes. Tight finishes are the norm (3 of 4 editions decided by 1 shot). Players who fold under Sunday pressure are not viable contenders
Pure links novices in calm conditions
Ironically, in calm conditions, links experience matters LESS — Tom Kim won his T3 debut here. The wind novice's disadvantage essentially disappears in low wind
